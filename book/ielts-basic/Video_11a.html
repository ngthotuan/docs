2. Conversion Rules: From Clause to Phrase

Converting from a clause (used with because/although) to a phrase (used with because of/despite) can be challenging. Here are 5 common rules depending on the sentence structure:

Rule 1: The subjects of both clauses are the SAME

If the subject of the dependent clause is the same as the subject of the main clause, you can simply drop the subject and change the verb to V-ing.

Subject + Verb ➔ V-ing

Example 1:

He had an accident because he drove too fast.

because he drove too fast ➔ because of driving too fast

Example 2:

She stayed at home because she felt unwell.

because she felt unwell ➔ because of feeling unwell

Rule 2: Subject is "It" + Verb + Adverb

If the dependent clause follows the structure "It + V + Adv" (often used for weather), convert it to a noun phrase: change "It" to "The", convert the Verb to a Noun, and convert the Adverb to an Adjective placed before the noun.

It + V + Adv ➔ The + Adj + Noun

Example:

The match was cancelled because it rained heavily.

because it rained heavily ➔ because of the heavy rain

Rule 3: Subject is a Pronoun + Verb + Adverb

If the subject is a personal pronoun (he, she, they, we...), convert the pronoun to a possessive adjective (his, her, their, our...), the verb to a noun, and the adverb to an adjective.

Pronoun + V + Adv ➔ Possessive Adj + Adj + Noun

Example:

She got promoted because she contributed greatly to our company.

because she contributed greatly ➔ because of her great contributions

Rule 4: Subject is a Pronoun + "To be" + Adjective

If the clause describes a characteristic using "to be", drop the "to be" verb, change the pronoun to a possessive adjective, and convert the adjective into a noun.

Pronoun + to be + Adj ➔ Possessive Adj + Noun

Example:

I like her because she is beautiful.

because she is beautiful ➔ because of her beauty

Rule 5: Subject is a Noun + "To be" + Adjective

If the subject is a noun (e.g., the weather, the salary), change the structure to "The + Adjective + Noun" and drop the "to be" verb.

The + Noun + to be + Adj ➔ The + Adj + Noun

Example 1:

We went for a walk because the weather was nice.

because the weather was nice ➔ because of the nice weather

Example 2:

Although the salary was low, he decided to accept the job.

Although the salary was low ➔ Despite the low salary

3. Important Tips & Warnings

⚠️ Warning: Do not use "still" with Although/Though/Even though

In Vietnamese, we often say "Mặc dù... nhưng vẫn..." (Although... still...). In English, the conjunctions of concession (although, despite, etc.) already carry the meaning of contrast. Adding "still" in the main clause is unnatural or redundant in most cases.

Vietnamese: Chúng tôi vẫn ra ngoài mặc dù trời mưa to.

  • ✗ Incorrect: We still went out although it rained heavily.
  • ✓ Correct: We went out although it rained heavily.

💡 Passive Voice in V-ing Phrase

If the verb in the dependent clause is in the passive voice (was/were + V3/ed) and you apply Rule 1 (same subject), the gerund phrase must be in the passive form: being + V3/ed.

  • Although he behaved impolitely, he wasn't punished.
  • ➔ Despite behaving impolitely, he wasn't punished.

Note: the passive verb "wasn't punished" remains intact in the main clause, but if the dependent clause was passive, e.g., "because they were tired" ➔ "because of being tired".
